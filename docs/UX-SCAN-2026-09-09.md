# UX scan — the whole interface

**Date:** 9 September 2026 · **Branch:** `fix/usability-2026-09` · **Scope:** all 86 Figma
screens on `p0zcsZTobPL8zZAeYhyYBt` / page `v4 - Complete`, against all 93 built
routes under `src/app/[locale]/`.

**Read `docs/AUDIT-2026-09-08.md` first.** That audit answered *what is unwired*.
This one answers a different question and does not repeat it: **what does a
person see, and can they tell what to do.** Where the two overlap I say so and
move on.

**Method.** Every claim below is either a Figma node id, a file path with a line
number, a grep count run on the repository at commit `59c6685`, or a string
quoted verbatim out of `src/i18n/messages/en.json`. Nothing is inferred from
memory. Figma did not rate-limit; every screen named in `docs/FIGMA_NODES.md`
was reachable, and the eleven I opened pixel-for-pixel are named in the text.

**The standard, in Abdou's words, applied to every screen:**

> A screen must say **what it is for**, **what state it is in right now**, and
> **what the person can do next**. A screen that only displays rows has failed,
> even when every row is correct.

## 0. What this scan changes about the framing

Three things in the brief for this scan turn out not to be true, and the real
shape is more useful than the assumed one.

**1. The design was not "10 items in 6 groups". The rail was never small.**
`_Shell` (`52:3`) and every one of the 86 frames that instances it draws **22
rows in 5 groups**: Today · Inbox · Conversations · Waiting on · Deals ·
Dashboard, then **WORK** (Tenders, Sourcing, Offers, Orders, Deliveries,
Projects), **MONEY** (Invoices, Payments), **COMPANIES & PEOPLE** (Companies,
Contacts, People, Personnel requests), **CONTROL** (Compliance, Files, Reports,
Settings).

The build (`src/components/layout/nav-items.ts`) has **23 rows in 5 groups**.
It removed Dashboard and Files and added Scan, Capture and Approvals. Net: one
row worse than a design that was already one row too many for a 768px laptop —
and the sidebar's own comment concedes it: *"Twenty-four destinations do not fit
in the 768 pixels of a 12-inch laptop."* The fix applied was `overflow-y-auto`.

And there is a third rail, older than both. `docs/UI_INDEX.md` — the written
record of what the v4 file was drawn to be — specifies **18 rows** and says so
explicitly:

```
Inbox (12)          <- first, badged
Dashboard
WORK           Enquiries . Tenders . Sourcing . Offers . Orders . Deliveries . Projects
MONEY          Invoices . Payments
RELATIONSHIPS  Companies . Contacts
PEOPLE         Candidates . Personnel requests
CONTROL        Compliance . Reports . Settings
```

> "Users & roles, Modules and Audit log sit inside Settings as tabs, so the
> sidebar stays at 18 items."

So the specification says 18, the master component it was drawn against draws
22, and the build ships 23. **Nobody has implemented this rail, including the
design file.** That is why item 14 in section 6 says do not restore the Figma
version.

So the rail is not a case of the build drifting from a good design. **The design
is wrong here and the build is wrong by the same amount.** Nobody should
redesign the rail *back* toward the Figma file. Both need replacing, and section
6 says with what.

**2. Nothing was designed and never built.** All 86 screens have a route on
disk. All 20 supporting routes in `docs/SCREENS.md` exist. All 93 `page.tsx`
files map to a documented screen. The coverage check in `SCREENS.md` runs in
both directions and it passes. **There is no missing screen in this ERP.**

**3. The drift is entirely *inside* the screens, and it is always the same
missing element: the verb.** The design's screens are built out of rows that
end in an action. The build's screens are built out of rows that end in a badge.
That is the whole finding, and it is measurable:

| | Design (v4) | Build |
|---|---|---|
| Offers list header | `Export` · **`New offer`** | *(no header action at all)* |
| Offers list state line | "7 awaiting client decision · 4 130 000 DZD at stake" | "7 offers · 4 still drafts" |
| Offers list facets | All 41 · Draft 4 · Sent 7 · Accepted 9 · Rejected 12 · Expired 9 | *(none)* |
| Offers table row | ends in a `⋯` overflow menu | ends in a date |
| Inbox row | **`Create enquiry`** · `Dismiss` | **`Create enquiry`** · `Dismiss` ✅ |
| Orders row "Nothing delivered" | *(design has a row menu)* | a red badge, and nothing to click |
| Enquiry header | `No-bid` · `Ask suppliers` · **`Build offer`** | six identical grey buttons, no primary |
| Enquiry banner | red sentence **+ `Record submission plan`** | red sentence, no button |
| Dashboard banner | "3 deadlines inside 48 hours · 2 invoices past 90 days · 1 caution expires in 11 days" **+ `Open the action list`** | *(no banner; no greeting; no header action; nothing clickable but four tiles)* |

**The count that matters.** Of the 30 list-shaped screens in the build, **20
have no action on any row** — Offers, Orders, Deliveries, Projects, Invoices,
Companies, Contacts, People, Candidates, Personnel requests, Compliance, Files,
Waiting on, Week, Reports, Search, Sourcing, Deals, Tenders, Audit log. Twelve
screens have no action in the page header either. Only **Today**, **Inbox**,
**Notifications**, **Approvals**, **Ageing**, **Compliance expiring**, **Users**,
**Bin**, **Payments** and **Conversations** put a verb on a row at all.

## 2. The pattern layer

**This is the part to redesign. There are twelve building blocks, not 86
screens.** Nine of the twelve are already written as components in
`src/components/` and are simply not adopted; two are written and have zero
consumers; one does not exist. Fixing the blocks fixes the screens.

---

### 2.1 The page header

**Design.** A named frame, `page-header`, 86px tall, present on every screen,
with exactly three slots (`59:994`):

- `ph-titles/ph:title` — the record or list name, in a 29px text box (≈24px type)
- `ph-titles/ph:sub` — one line of **live state**, 16px box
- `ph:actions` — right-aligned, 32px tall, one to three buttons, **exactly one
  primary**

Above it, a **three-level breadcrumb**: `SUPSERV / Enquiries / ENQ-2026-0141`.

**Build.** There is no `PageHeader` component. The markup
`border-b border-line-subtle bg-surface px-4 md:px-7 py-5` is **copy-pasted into
66 files**. The `h1` is hardcoded `text-[19px]` in **94 places** — five pixels
smaller than the design and outside the token scale entirely. The actions slot
is absent on 12 screens. The breadcrumb (`topbar.tsx:76`) is **two levels only**
and derives from `NAV_GROUPS`, so:

- it **never names the record** — `/deals/9f2c…` reads `SUPSERV / Deals`
- on any route not in the rail it shows the word **SUPSERV alone**: `/setup`,
  `/dashboard`, `/week`, `/search`, `/files`, `/notifications`, `/assistant`,
  `/documents/[id]`, `/purchase-orders/[id]`

**Divergence.** The design is right and the build lost the actions slot, the
third crumb, and 5px of type. This one component is the highest-leverage object
in the whole scan: it is the only thing on screen that can be *required* to
carry a state line and an action.

**Fixed version.**

```
<PageHeader
  crumb={[{label, href}, …]}   // ends in the record; required
  title={…}                    // required
  state={…}                    // required — live, computed, never a description
  actions={…}                  // REQUIRED PROP. Pass `null` and a lint rule
/>                             // makes you write why, in a comment, once.
```

The `actions` prop being required is the whole design decision. A component you
*can* leave out is one that will be left out 12 times.

### 2.2 The list

**Design (screen 79, `127:2`).** The frame states its own scope: *"'Filters' and
'Columns' appear as buttons on ten list screens… Deals, Tenders, Sourcing,
Offers, Orders, Projects, Invoices, Companies, Candidates, People."* It
specifies a facet chip row with counts, a filter panel, saved views ("a named
question, not a query"), a columns menu, a bulk bar, a pagination footer, and
**four rules that keep filtering honest**:

1. the count always shows both — "14 of 342"
2. the filter lives in the address
3. an empty result shows the filters that caused it, each removable on its own
4. a hidden row says so

**Build.** `src/components/data/` implements all of it, faithfully, including
the URL encoding (`use-list-state.ts`, `stage:a|b,owner:me`). Rules 1, 2 and 4
are honoured. **Rule 3 is not implemented anywhere in the ERP** —
`data-table.tsx:55` computes `isFiltered` and passes it only to `Pagination`
(line 223); the empty branch at line 112 renders the same `emptyState` whether
you have no records or a filter that matched nothing.

Adoption: **5 of the 10 named screens** use `DataTable` — Companies, Contacts,
Deals, People, Tenders. **55 files still write a raw `<table>`.**

**Divergence.** The design is right; the build built it and then stopped using
it. This is not a redesign job, it is an adoption job — except for rule 3, which
is a genuine gap.

**Fixed version.** `DataTable` gains a second empty slot:

```
emptyState        // no records at all — names what goes here + the verb
noMatchState      // filters returned nothing — lists the applied filters as
                  // removable chips + `Clear all`
rowAction         // ← the new one. See 2.6.
```

Adopt on Sourcing, Offers, Orders, Projects, Invoices, Candidates next. Leave
the six single-purpose tables (setup steps, ageing buckets, BPU grid, document
lines, audit log, scorecard) alone — they are not lists of records.

### 2.3 The state line

**Design.** Every list header's second line names a **stake**, not a count:
"7 awaiting client decision · **4 130 000 DZD at stake**". The dashboard tiles
do the same: "Overdue receivables · 8 240 000 DZD · *2 invoices past 90 days*".

**Build.** 33 of the 60 header subtitles are live and computed — genuinely good
work, and several are better written than the design:

> `/tenders` — *"12 open · 2 closing within 48 hours · 3 folders incomplete"*
> `/contacts` — *"41 people at 18 companies · 6 unverified · 2 emails bouncing"*
> `/deliveries` — *"14 delivery notes · 3 without a signed copy"*

And one is a count of itself: `/companies` prints `common.showing` with `shown`

screen genuinely has no state (Modules, Assistant safety, Forms) it says so in
those words — those three already do, and they are the model.

### 2.4 Empty, loading, error

**Design (screen 34, `67:2`).** Five states, each with a stated rule:

| State | The design's own rule |
|---|---|
| Empty | "name what goes here and give the action that creates it" — **two buttons**: `Go to Inbox` + **`New enquiry`** |
| Filtered empty | "show the filters that caused it and let one be removed" — `Remove deadline filter` + `Clear all` |
| Loading | "skeleton in the real column shape — no layout shift" |
| Error | "say what failed and that nothing changed, then offer Retry" — *"Could not load deals / The server did not respond. Nothing was changed."* + `Retry` |
| No permission | "show the page, hide the field, name the permission" — *"Ask the Gérant for offers.margin.view."* |

**Build.**

- `StateBlock` exists with three tones and an `action` slot. Consumers went from
  2 (at the September audit) to **9**. Real progress.
- **Filtered empty: 0 implementations.** A repo-wide grep for
  `noMatch|noResults|filteredEmpty|clearAll|removeFilter` returns nothing.
- **Loading: one file for the entire application** — `(app)/loading.tsx` renders

  grid, the setup wizard and every form. That is the "layout shift" the design
  forbids, applied globally.
- **Error: one file** — `(app)/error.tsx`. No per-route boundaries, so a failure
  in the ageing query blanks the whole page rather than the one card.
- **Not found: zero files.** There is no `not-found.tsx` anywhere. The
  `deletedPartyNotice` rule ("never a blank 404, say what used to be here") has

- `StateBlock` has no icon; the design gives each state one.

**Fixed version.** `StateBlock` gains `icon` and a `filtered` tone; a
`loading.tsx` per shape (list / detail / form / document); an `error.tsx` at the
three sub-trees that can fail independently; one `not-found.tsx` carrying the
deleted-record sentence.

### 2.5 The empty state is better than the populated state — and that is backwards

This is worth stating on its own because it is the most counter-intuitive
finding in the scan.

On `/offers`, `/orders`, `/tenders`, `/sourcing`, `/deliveries` and
`/payments`, **the only primary button on the screen lives inside the
`rows.length === 0` branch.** The moment the company has data — which is the
moment the screen matters — the button disappears and the screen becomes a
table.

A new person on a demo database sees the good version of these screens. Abdou,
on the real one, never does. That inversion is a large part of why the system
feels different when described and when used.

### 2.6 The card with row actions

**Design.** Every row that reports a state also carries the verb that changes
it. Three frames prove it is a system and not a flourish:

- **Inbox (`55:2`)** — each row ends in a verb chosen by kind:
  `Create enquiry` · `Create candidate` · `Attach to sourcing` · `Create task` ·
  `Link to invoice` · `Classify`, each with `Dismiss` beside it.
- **Enquiry detail (`58:416`)** — the *Sourcing status* card lists four
  suppliers with `Replied` / `Waiting 3d` / `Declined` chips and ends with
  **`Chase 2 suppliers`** + `Add supplier`. The *Files* card ends with `Upload` ·
  `Open in OneDrive`.
- **Dashboard (`56:2`)** — *Money at risk* ends with `Open ageing report` +
  **`Send relances (2)`**; *Data blocking work* rows carry a red `Cannot
  invoice` chip.

**Build.** Ten screens do this. Twenty do not. The verbs mostly **exist as
working server actions somewhere else in the app**:

| The row that says the problem | The verb that would fix it | Where it lives today |
|---|---|---|
| `/waiting-on` — supplier quiet 9 days | `Chase {n}` | `/sourcing/[id]` only |
| `/orders` — `Nothing delivered` | `Record delivery` | `/deliveries/new` only |

| `/invoices` — 94 days overdue | `Relance` | `/payments/ageing` only |

docstring: **a badge that names a problem must be accompanied by the verb that
ends it, or by a greyed control with the reason (2.11).** `/today`'s
`gather()` already produces exactly this shape — `{ title, detail, href, action

be the shared one.

### 2.11 The greyed control and its reason

**Design (screen 80, `129:2`).** No grey button without a reason. The popover
names the rule, its authority, and the route that fixes it.

**Build.** Both halves exist and they are not connected.

- `Button` takes `disabledReason` and deliberately uses `aria-disabled` rather
  than `disabled` *"so nobody could ever read the reason it is grey"*. **33
  files pass it** — real progress since the audit.
- The reason is then rendered as `title={disabledReason}` — **a native browser
  tooltip**. It appears after about a second of mouse hover. It never appears on
  a touch device. It is not announced to a screen reader (there is no
  `aria-describedby`). On a 12-inch laptop, in practice, **the reason is
  invisible**.
- `RulePopover` — the component written to render exactly this, with authority
  and fix route — is imported by **zero files**. It has been unused since it was
  written.

**Divergence.** The design is right, the build has both pieces, and the wire
between them was never run. This is perhaps a hundred lines of work for a rule
the whole system claims to obey.

**Fixed version.** `Button` renders `disabledReason` through `RulePopover` when
the reason resolves to a `blocking_rule`, and through a visible inline
`<span class="text-micro text-muted">` beside the button when it does not. Never
`title` alone.

### 2.12 The form

**Design.** Labelled fields, required marked, inline errors, one primary submit,
`Discard` beside it.

**Build.** `src/app/[locale]/(app)/setup/field.tsx` exports `INPUT` (a class
string) and `Field` (label + required marker + hint).

- **36 files import `INPUT`.**
- **6 files import `Field`.**
- **45 files write a raw `<label>`.**
- **`aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby` appear zero times in the entire app.**

So there is no field-level error anywhere. Every validation failure is a
redirect to `?error=…` and a page-level red paragraph — **205 such redirects
across 53 action files, read back by 52 pages**. A person who mistypes an NIF is
told, at the top of the page, that something is wrong; nothing marks the field.

The error also **stays in the address bar**, so a refresh or a back-button
re-shows an error about something already fixed.

**Fixed version.** `Field` gains `error` and wires `aria-invalid` +
`aria-describedby`; server actions return `{ field, code }` rather than a query
string; the `?error=` redirect stays only for the whole-page refusals
(`?blocked=issued`, `setupIncomplete`) where it is correct.

### 2.14 Type, and the reason everything looks flat

Not in the brief, and it is the cheapest large improvement available.

`src/app/globals.css` defines five sizes: `micro 11.5 · tiny 12 · small 12.5 ·
base 13.5 · lead 14.5`. Across `src/app`:

| token | uses |
|---|---|
| `text-micro` (11.5px) | **963** |
| `text-tiny` (12px) | **1091** |
| `text-small` (12.5px) | 4 |
| `text-base` (13.5px) | 14 |
| `text-lead` (14.5px) | 12 |

**2 054 of 2 084 text instances are 12px or smaller.** Card headings use
`text-tiny` — *the same size as body text*, separated only by weight. The page
`h1` is not in the scale at all: it is `text-[19px]`, hardcoded in 94 files.

Measured from the Figma frames: body text sits in 16px boxes (≈13px type),
card headings in 17px boxes (≈14px), page titles in 29px boxes (≈24px). So the
design has three distinct levels above body and the build has one, five pixels
short.

There is no dark mode and no need for one. There are also **no bound Figma
variables on `v4 - Complete`** — `get_variable_defs` on a screen returns `{}`,
and `docs/DECISIONS/2026-08-19-figma-componentisation.md` explains why. So
`globals.css` is the design system of record, and it is missing exactly two
tokens.

**Fixed version.** Add `--text-section: 14.5px` (reuse `lead`) and
`--text-title: 24px`; move `h1` onto the token; move every card `h2` off
`text-tiny`; raise body from 12 to 13. Four lines of CSS and a find-replace.
Nothing else in this scan changes the *feel* of the whole product for less work.

## 3. The flow, named

The design already names it, on screen 37 `Flow map — where every record is born
and where it ends` (`71:2`), with its own legend: *"Green means the UI can
capture it today. Red means the work arrives this way and has nowhere to go."*
**Nothing in the built application points at that frame, and no screen in the
ERP draws the chain.** A person standing anywhere in the system cannot see the
line they are on.

### 3.1 The main chain — mail → money

The flow map's pipeline, and the screen that carries each step:

| # | Step | Screen carrying it | "Where am I?" | "What came before?" | "What happens next?" |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Received | `/inbox` | ✓ named | — | ✓ row verb `Create enquiry` |
| 2 | Classified | `/inbox` type chip + "needs a human" card | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ two binary buttons |
| 3 | Becomes a deal | `/inbox/[id]` → commit | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| 4 | Go / no-go | `/deals/[id]` decision card | ~ | **✗ break 1** | ✓ stepper step 1 |
| 5 | Sourcing | `/deals/[id]` ask card → `/sourcing/[id]` | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ stepper |
| 6 | Priced | `/deals/[id]/prices` | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ stepper |
| 7 | Offer drafted | `/offers/[id]/build` | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ `submitChecks` |
| 8 | Submitted + proof | `/documents/[id]` issue → send | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| 9 | **Client decision** | **no screen** | **✗ break 3** | — | **✗** |
| 10 | Order | `/documents/[id]/convert?target=client_order`, linked only from `/deals/[id]` | ~ | ~ | **✗ break 4** |
| 11 | Delivery | `/deliveries/new?source=…`, linked only from `/documents/[id]` | ✓ | ✓ | **✗ break 5** |
| 12 | Invoice | `/invoices/new?source=…`, linked only from `/documents/[id]` | ✓ | ✓ | **✗ break 6** |
| 13 | Payment | `/payments` record form | ✓ | **✗ break 7** | ~ |

The good news first: **`dealChecks` in `src/domain/deal/checks.ts` already models
the whole chain** — `decision · lines · deadline · suppliers · prices · offer ·
clientAnswer · delivery · invoice` — and renders it as a numbered stepper on
`/deals/[id]`. On the deal, and only on the deal, a person can see where they
are. Every other screen in the chain is silent about it.

**The seven breaks, precisely:**

**Break 1 — a deal cannot see the email it came from.**
`src/domain/intake/commit.ts:264` writes `deal.intakeMessageId`. It is read by
**no screen**. `/deals/[id]/page.tsx` contains no link to `/inbox`, no
attachment list, no "opened from this message". So the first thing a new person
asks — *"where did this come from?"* — has no answer on the screen where they
ask it. (Audit §2 named the same column; this is its interface consequence.)

**Break 2 — the deal's own facts are write-once.**
`/deals/new` captures `clientReference`, `deadlineAt`, `submissionMethod`,
`currency`, `clientInstructions`. `deals/[id]/actions.ts` exports exactly three
actions: `decideAction`, `lostAction`, `reopenAction`. **There is no
`/deals/[id]/edit`.** `dealChecks` line 153 pushes
`{ key: "deadline", state: "warn" }` **with no `fixHref`**, and the comment above
it says why: no screen does it. So a deadline read wrong off an email can only
be corrected by discarding the deal and retyping it. This is the single most
likely thing to happen in the first ten minutes of anyone's use.

**Break 3 — "the client said yes" is not an event.**
`checks.ts:221` — `if (facts.offersIssued > 0 && facts.ordersReceived === 0)`
pushes `clientAnswer` as a `note` with no link. That is correct reasoning (won
is derived from a client order arriving, per LAW 1) and a hole in the
experience: between sending an offer and a bon de commande turning up, which is
usually weeks, **the ERP shows nothing changing and offers nothing to do.**
There is no "record what they said", no "chase the decision", no expiry warning
on the offer's own validity. The design has this: the Offers list carries a
`Sent` status, a `Validity` column that reads `expired`, and a `Why we lost`
panel driven by *"reason is required when an offer is marked lost"*. None of
those three exist in the build.

**Break 4 — the offer does not lead to the order.**
`/offers` links to `/deals` and `/offers/[id]/build`. `/offers/[id]/build` links
to `/deals/[id]`, `/documents/[id]` and `/documents/[id]/edit`. Neither links to
`convert?target=client_order`. The conversion is reachable **only** by going back
to the deal page and finding the right card in a stack of fourteen.

**Break 5 — the order does not lead to the delivery.**
`/orders` rows render a red `Nothing delivered` badge and link to the document.
`Record delivery` exists at `/deliveries/new` and is not offered on the row that
says it is needed.

**Break 6 — nothing leads to the invoice, and the code says so honestly.**
`checks.ts:259` pushes `{ key: "invoice", state: "warn" }` with no `fixHref`,
with a reasoned comment: a facture is raised *from a document*, and the deal does
not know which. The reasoning is right; the answer is wrong. The fix is not a
link — it is a **picker**: "invoice which of these two documents?", listing the
bon de commande and the bons de livraison the deal already has.

**Break 7 — a payment does not lead back to its invoice.**
`/payments` links to `/payments/ageing` and `/settings/compliance` and to no
document, deal or company. The payment is allocated against invoices in the
domain; the screen never shows you which.

**Break 8 — `/purchase-orders/[id]` (screen 68) is an island.** 733 lines, three
primary actions, the three-way match against the supplier invoice — and exactly
one inbound link in the entire application, from a conditional in an `/orders`
row (`orders/page.tsx:148`).

### 3.3 What would make the chain visible

Not a diagram. Three things, all of which reuse code that exists:

1. **The stepper on every record in the chain**, not only the deal (§2.7). The

2. **A "came from / goes to" line under every record's title.** `ENQ-2026-0141`

3. **Forward verbs on the rows that need them** (§2.6): offer → order, order →

## 4. Ten minutes with someone who has never seen it

A new commercial. A laptop. Nobody sitting beside them. This is what happens.

**0:00 — `/sign-in`.** Good. *"Sign in / Use the Microsoft 365 account you
already sign in to email with. SUPSERV keeps no password of its own."* One
button, `Continue with Microsoft`. If they are refused: *"Only accounts in the
SUPSERV directory can sign in. If yours is refused, ask the Gérant to add
you."* This screen is finished.

If nobody has given them a role, they get the best refusal in the system:
*"Your account works, but you have no access yet… Nothing is broken — ask them
to open Settings → Users and roles."* Also finished — and it hides a trap.

**There is no invite.** `docs/UI_INDEX.md` lists *"Editable permission matrix +
invite"* against screen 30; the matrix was deliberately made read-only (correct
— `src/auth/can.ts` is the single source) and **invite was never built**.
`/settings/users` lists people who have already signed in and lets a Gérant set
each one's role from a `<select>`. So the only way to add the second person to
this ERP is: they sign in, they are refused, they walk to the Gérant, the Gérant
opens Settings → Users and roles, sets the role, and they sign in again. The
refusal screen is written as though someone could be added in advance. Nobody
can.

**0:20 — they land on `/today`.** `src/app/[locale]/page.tsx` redirects here,
which is the right destination on day 400 and the wrong one on day 1. On an
empty database Today says:

> **Tuesday 9 September**
> *Nothing needs a decision from you today.*
> ✓ **Nothing needs a decision from you**
> *Nothing is waiting on anybody else either.*
> *Done today — Nothing recorded yet today.*
> *Later this week — Nothing else has a date this week.*

Four statements that the system is empty, and **no route out of it**. The two
controls in the header are `Quick capture` and `Week ahead`. The word "setup"
does not appear. **`/setup` is not in the rail, and nothing on this page links
to it.** A grep of `src/app` for a redirect or link to `/setup` finds six —
Compliance, Dashboard (demoted out of the rail), `/documents/new`,
`/settings/compliance`, `/settings`, and `/setup`'s own sub-pages. On a fresh
system, **every one of those is at least two clicks away and none is signposted.**

This is the whole first-run problem in one screen. The system's best surface
tells a new person that there is nothing to do, on the day when there are eleven
things to do and the software knows exactly what they are.

**1:00 — they look at the rail.** Twenty-three rows in five groups. The three
group headings are `WORK`, `MONEY`, `COMPANIES & PEOPLE`, `CONTROL` — plus six
ungrouped rows at the top with no heading at all. Nothing on the rail says which
of the twenty-three is the beginning. `Today` and `Inbox` carry badges reading
`0`; the rest are identical grey rows.

The first group is the hardest to read: Today · Inbox · **Scan station** ·
**Quick capture** · Conversations · Waiting on · Deals. Three of those seven
(Scan station, Quick capture, Conversations) are ways *in*; two (Waiting on,
Today) are ways of *looking*; one is a *list of records*. They are grouped by
nothing.

**1:30 — they click `Deals`, because it sounds like the main thing.** An empty
list with `New deal` in the header. They click it. `/deals/new` asks for: client
(a `<select>` — with an empty company table it has no options), client reference,
subject, deadline, submission method, currency, client instructions, and a paste
box. Nine fields with no company to attach them to. There is a link to
`/companies/new`, which is correct, and the trip is: Deals → New deal → New
company → back.

Two things go wrong here that they will not discover for weeks:

- Nothing on this form says a deal can also be **made from an email**, which is
  the way it is actually supposed to happen (screen 02's row verb). The subtitle
  is *"A deal arrived. Record what they asked for before it goes cold."*
- The deadline they type here is **the last time they will ever be able to type
  it.** There is no `/deals/[id]/edit` (§3, break 2).

**2:30 — the deal is created and they land on `/deals/[id]`.** The header is
`ENQ-2026-0141 — <subject>`, and the rail row they came from said **Deals**, and
the French says **Affaires**. **Three names for one record, one of them printed
on the record itself.** This is the naming drift the September audit found;
the copy has since been fixed (the English body says "deal" 128 times against
"enquiry" 4) but the *identifier* was not, so the mismatch is now more visible,
not less.

Below the title: **six identical grey buttons** — Timeline, Prices, Technical,
Items, Folder, BPU. No primary. Nothing is highlighted. A new person reads six
equal choices and picks none.

Then **fourteen stacked cards**. Somewhere among them is the "what happens next"
stepper, which is the one thing on the page that answers their question — and it
is in the right-hand column, below the fold on a 1366×768 laptop, under
`Details`. The design puts the equivalent (`Sourcing status`, with `Chase 2
suppliers`) in the same rail but above the fold, because the design has six
cards and not fourteen.

**4:00 — they try Settings, because nothing has told them what to do.** This is
the screen Abdou singled out, and it is now the best-organised page in the ERP
(§5). Twenty-three rows, each with a name, a sentence and a live chip, in five
groups each with its own sentence. The banner at the top says:

> *No document can be issued yet: legal identity, bank account, VAT rate,
> numbering still to do. Everything else on this page works without them.*

**That banner names the problem and has no button.** The one control on the
whole screen is `Day one checklist`, top right, in `variant="secondary"` — grey,
the same weight as everything else. This is the moment where a new person either
finds the front door or does not, and the front door is drawn as a footnote.

**4:30 — if they press it, everything comes right.** `/setup` is the best screen
in the system for this purpose:

> **Day one** — *the empty system · eleven things to set before the first
> document can be issued*
> ⚠ *Until these are done, no invoice can be issued at all: legal identity, bank
> account, VAT rate, numbering. This is not a warning — the number allocator
> refuses.*
> **Before anything can be issued — 0 of 11 done**
> 1 Legal identity … [ **Set** ]  2 Logo … [ Set ]  3 Bank account … [ Set ] …

Numbered, computed, one primary button on the step that is blocking, a green
`Done` badge on the ones finished, and a panel headed *"What the system will not
invent for you"*. **Everything a new person needs is here.** It is behind a grey
button on a screen they had no reason to open.

**6:00 — they finish identity and press Save.** They land back on `/setup` with
`?saved=identity` and a green line. Good. If they typed an NIF wrong, they land
on `/setup/identity?error=…` with a red paragraph at the top of the page and
**no mark on the field** — there is no `aria-invalid` anywhere in this
application (§2.12). They have to re-read nine fields to find the one.

**7:00 — they go back to Today.** Still empty; `/setup` progress does not appear
on it. They try `Inbox`: *"Nothing waiting — Everything that arrived has been
dealt with. New mail appears here when the mailbox is synced."* with `Sync now`.
Good screen. They press it and, if the mailbox is scoped, mail arrives with a
`Create enquiry` button on every row. **This is the moment the ERP explains
itself** — and it is seven minutes and four wrong turns in.

**8:00 — they wander.** `Tenders`: a table they cannot act on. `Sourcing`: the
same. `Offers`: the same, with a header that has no buttons at all. `Orders`: a
row that says `Nothing delivered` in red and does nothing when clicked.
`Waiting on`: a blue banner saying *"Nothing on this page needs you today"*
whether or not anything is waiting (`waiting-on/page.tsx:50`). `Reports`:
*"Everything is computed when the page is drawn. No total is stored."*
`Week ahead`: dates with no verbs. `Files`: forty-two words about where files
come from.

Eight screens in a row that display and do not offer. **This is the exact
experience behind "there's a lot of screens with no real actions", and it is
literally true: twenty of the thirty list screens have no action on any row.**

**10:00 — what they would say if you asked.** Not "it's missing features". They
would say they could not tell where to start, that most screens looked the same,
that everything was the same size and colour, and that the software kept
describing itself instead of telling them what to do.

### The three fixes that change this ten minutes

1. **When the system is empty, `/today` shows the day-one stepper** instead of
   "nothing needs you". Same component, same computed steps, and `setupState()`
   already knows. One screen, half a day.
2. **`/deals/[id]/edit`**, so the first typo is not permanent.
3. **A primary button in every page header** (§2.1) — the single change that
   turns eight dead screens into eight live ones.

### 5.3 What is wrong with the screen as a whole

**1. One button, and it is grey.** `Day one checklist`, `variant="secondary"`,
top right. On an unconfigured system this is the most important control in the
application and it is drawn at the weight of a tab. The red banner beneath it —
*"No document can be issued yet: legal identity, bank account, VAT rate,
numbering still to do."* — **names four blockers and offers no way to fix any of
them.** Each of those four is a row on the same page, twelve inches lower.

**2. The subtitle is already stale.** The build says *"The company, the money,
the documents and what comes in"* — four things, on a page with five groups, one
of which is about what to do when something goes wrong. The approved frame
`166:14459` carries the better line: *"Everything this system holds about the
company, the people, what comes in — and what to do when something goes
wrong."* The frame is one day old and the build has not taken it.

**3. Three message strings are dead.** `settings.entry.storage.unbuilt`,
`settings.entry.compliance.unbuilt` and `settings.entry.audit.unbuilt` are
written in both languages and **no entry sets `unbuilt: true` any more** — a grep
for it in `src/app` returns nothing. They describe a state the screen can no
longer be in.

**4. Twenty-three rows and no way to find one.** No search box, no filter, and
the groups run down two unequal columns (nine rows on the left against three on
the right). The design's answer was tabs. Neither answer is right for
twenty-three rows; a search field over the row names and their sentences is,
and the `⌘K` palette (§2.13) would cover it for free.

**5. The one class of setting that Abdou asked about is not here at all.**

He said: *"You talk about backup — there's no settings for backup, or to change
the folder. What does the backup do? How does it work? Is it daily? And the
documents expiration, it's not clear."*

Backup was fixed (U1) and expiry was fixed (U2). **The general case was not.**
The ERP's behaviour is governed by at least seventeen hardcoded thresholds, none
of which is visible or settable anywhere:

single answer to "a lot of things are not clear": the system has opinions about
time and does not tell anyone what they are.

The first version does not have to be editable. **A read-only "How this system
counts time" panel, listing every threshold with the screen it governs, would
close most of the complaint** — and it is one file reading fourteen exported
constants.

## 7. What must survive this

Repeating the audit's list only where this scan confirmed it from the interface
side, plus what has been built since:

1. **`/today`** — four ordered bands, every row ending in a verb, and a closing
   card that names what is deliberately *not* on the page. It is the reference
   implementation of §2.6 and nothing in this document should change it.
2. **`/setup`** — computed steps, one primary on the blocker, a banner that
   names exactly what is missing. The model for §2.7.
3. **`/inbox`** — the only list where the row carries the verb, with
   `disabledReason` on the case where it cannot.
4. **The settings hub as U3 left it** — name, sentence, live chip, destination,
   on all 23 rows, in groups that each say what they are for. Copy this pattern;
   do not replace it.
5. **`/settings/backup` (U1) and `/compliance/expiring` (U2)** — both are
   direct, complete answers to things Abdou said were unclear, and both are
   better than their frames.
6. **The writing.** The copy in this system is unusually good and it is not the
   problem. `waiting.thresholdNote`, `orders.none`, `bulk-bar`'s rule about what
   is never offered in bulk, `conversations.cannotSend`, `search.notLookedYet` —
   these are better than most commercial software. **Do not rewrite copy in this
   pass.** The failure is that the sentences explain and the screens do not act.
7. **Computed state, never stored.** Every proposal here is computable from rows
   that exist. Nothing in this document needs a `progress`, `stage` or `task`
   column, and nothing should get one.
